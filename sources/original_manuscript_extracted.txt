Abstract
The sociological intricacies of nature have inspired numerous optimization algorithms, providing computation-
ally efficient heuristics for solving NP-hard, high-dimensional problems where brute-force methods are imprac-
tical. Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO), a bio-inspired metaheuristic, mimics hierarchical social structures
in elephant clans to balance exploration and exploitation. This study introduces a modified EHO variant, in-
tegrating hierarchical influences to enhance optimization performance. A comparative analysis is conducted
against Genetic Algorithms (GA), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) on
benchmark problems such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) and Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP). Fur-
thermore, this work extends EHO’s application to medical imaging, specifically MRI slice enhancement and 3D
reconstruction, demonstrating its potential in computational medical diagnostics.
1. Introduction
Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO), a bio-inspired metaheuristic,
mimics hierarchical social structures to balance exploration and exploita-
tion in NP-hard problems 1. This study presents a modified EHO variant,
enhancing optimization performance. A comparative analysis against GA
2, ACO 3, and PSO4 is conducted on TSP and VRP benchmarks. Addition-
ally, EHO’ s application to MRI slice enhancement and 3D reconstruction
is explored, showcasing its potential in medical diagnostics.
2. Problem Statement
As optimization problems become more complex, traditional algorithms
struggle with high dimensionality, non-linearity, and noise. In medical
imaging, tasks like MRI enhancement and 3D reconstruction demand high
precision, yet existing methods often yield suboptimal results. This study
tackles these challenges by: (1) Benchmarking Elephant Herding Optimiza-
tion (EHO) against GA, PSO, and ACO on TSP and VRP; and (2) Exploring
EHO’s application in MRI slice enhancement and 3D reconstruction to as-
sess its impact on diagnostic accuracy.
3. Contributions
This research benchmarks EHO on TSP , VRP , Rastrigin, and Ackley,
demonstrating superior convergence and accuracy over brute-force, GA,
PSO, and ACO. It further introduces EHO for MRI slice enhancement and
3D reconstruction, proving its robustness in noisy conditions. Compar-
ative analysis highlights EHO’ s efficiency in execution time and solution
accuracy. Lastly, the study emphasizes EHO’ s potential in healthcare by
improving tumor detection and surgical planning through enhanced 3D
MRI reconstructions.

4.1 Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO) Algorithm

herds, where individuals represent candidate solutions. The population is
structured into herds to balance diversity and exploitation. Optimization
is driven by a competitive-collaborative approach with matriarch-led ex-
ploitation and clan-based global exploration.

5.2 Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP)
The V ehicle Routing Problem (VRP) extends the Traveling Salesman Prob-
lem (TSP) by incorporating multiple vehicles to serve customers while ad-
hering to capacity and time constraints. Unlike TSP , which seeks a single
optimal tour, VRP requires constructing multiple cost-minimizing routes
that collectively cover all customers. As an NP-hard problem, VRP plays
a critical role in logistics, transportation, and supply chain management,
where efficient routing directly impacts operational costs and resource uti-
lization. Let N be the total number of customers (including a depot), and
M the number of vehicles. Each customer i has a demand di, and each
vehicle v has a capacity Qv. The objective is to determine optimal routes

Here, D(Ci, Cj) represents the Euclidean distance between customers Ci

customer is visited once and no vehicle exceeds its capacity.
T o solve VRP using Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO), modifications
were introduced to handle multi-vehicle constraints and feasibility condi-
tions. Each elephant represented a candidate VRP solution, encoding mul-
tiple routes while ensuring vehicle capacities were not exceeded. The herd-
ing mechanism enhanced route efficiency and convergence, with herd-level
updates refining individual routes based on the best-performing solutions,
while global exploration leveraged the best-known solution across all herds
to optimize vehicle allocations dynamically. Adaptive weight adjustments
maintained a balance between exploration and exploitation, ensuring effi-
cient route optimization. For Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), veloc-
ity updates were adapted for VRP’ s discrete nature. Instead of continuous
adjustments, swaps and shifts between customer locations defined the par-
ticle updates, preserving solution feasibility. A customized cost function
evaluated solutions based on total travel cost while penalizing infeasible
assignments. Dynamic penalty adjustments prioritized feasible solutions,
ensuring adherence to vehicle capacity constraints. T o enable robust com-
parisons, EHO and PSO were systematically tuned by optimizing popula-
tion size, iteration limits, and weight coefficients.
5.2.1 Observations and Analysis
Experimental results indicate that EHO outperformed PSO in solving
VRP , achieving lower total costs while maintaining computational effi-

distributions. In a test case with 20 customers and 5 vehicles, EHO achieved
a total cost of 523.85, compared to 693.38 for PSO. Additionally, EHO
demonstrated a significantly lower execution time, reinforcing its viability
for large-scale VRP instances.

7. EHO for Medical Image Registration
Medical image registration plays a crucial role in computational medical
imaging, ensuring spatial alignment between images obtained from differ-
ent modalities, time points, or perspectives.
7.1 Definition of Medical Image Registration and Its Challenges
Medical image registration aligns images from different modalities, time
points, or perspectives, essential for disease monitoring, multi-modal fu-
sion, and surgical comparisons. It estimates a transformation function to
minimize discrepancies between a fixed reference and a moving image,
ensuring anatomical consistency. However, challenges arise from biolog-
ical variability, motion-induced deformations, and modality-specific dif-
ferences in contrast, resolution, and acquisition parameters, making accu-
rate registration computationally demanding. Traditional rigid registration
uses intensity-based measures like mutual information or cross-correlation
but struggles with structural differences. Deformable registration mod-
els complex transformations but faces challenges from high-dimensional
spaces and local minima. Robust optimization is crucial for efficient, reli-
able alignment, especially amid noise and artifacts.
7.2 The Impact of Random Noise on Medical Image Registration
Noise is an inherent issue in medical imaging, arising from acquisition pro-
tocols, hardware limitations, and patient motion. It disrupts pixel inten-
sities and structural coherence, complicating feature extraction and mis-
guiding optimization algorithms into converging at incorrect local optima.
Mathematically, it is modeled as an additive Gaussian perturbation, causing
intensity variations that distort similarity measures like normalized mutual
information and mean squared error (MSE), reducing registration accu-
racy. As noise inflates the variance of the dissimilarity function, by intro-
ducing spurious local minima, noise complicates optimization, particularly
in high-dimensional tasks where distinguishing true anatomical structures
from artifacts becomes challenging. Addressing these issues requires an
optimization framework that balances exploration and exploitation while
mitigating noise effects for robust image alignment.

7.5 Framework for Applying EHO in Medical Image Registration
Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO) optimized medical image registra-
tion by encoding transformation parameters (translation, rotation, scal-
ing) within a multidimensional space. Each elephant represented a can-
didate transformation, evaluated using Mean Squared Error (MSE). The
population was initialized for diversity and divided into herds, with lead-
ers guiding cooperative and competitive updates—aligning elephants with
their leader while pushing them toward the global best solution. Adap-
tive mutation and perturbation prevented premature convergence and im-
proved resilience against noise, ensuring robust exploration and optimal
alignment.
7.5.1 Adaptations for Noise-Induced Challenges
Medical image registration is affected by noise, distorting similarity metrics
and introducing local minima. T o counter this, EHO incorporated noise-
robust fitness evaluations, smoothing fitness values for better leader se-
lection. Adaptive mutation introduced controlled perturbations, prevent-
ing stagnation, while reinitialization preserved diversity, ensuring effective
search space exploration. These adaptations enhanced EHO’ s robustness in
noisy conditions.

8. EHO for MRI Image Enhancement and 3D Recon-
struction
Magnetic Resonance Imaging is essential in modern diagnostics, but raw
outputs often suffer from noise, low contrast, and motion artifacts, affecting
reliability.
8.1 Role of MRI Enhancement in Diagnostics and 3D Modeling
MRI provides high-resolution soft tissue imaging but suffers from noise,
low contrast, and motion artifacts, reducing diagnostic reliability. En-
hancement techniques refine images for better pathology detection, crucial
in oncology where tumor boundary clarity impacts clinical decisions. Tra-
ditional methods like histogram equalization and Gaussian filtering often
lead to over-smoothing or excessive contrast. In contrast, EHO dynami-
cally enhances pixel intensities, balancing contrast adjustments while pre-
serving critical details for accurate diagnosis.
8.1.1 Need for 3D Reconstruction in Comprehensive Analysis
2D MRI slices lack spatial continuity, making 3D reconstruction essen-
tial for accurate visualization in neurosurgery. Traditional interpolation
causes artifacts, distorting spatial relationships. EHO-driven optimization
preserves structural details, enhancing diagnostic accuracy and AI-driven
tumor classification.
8.1.2 Overview of the BRATS Dataset and Its Relevance
The BRATS dataset, with multimodal MRI scans and tumor annotations, is

8.2 Methodology for MRI Slice Enhancement
MRI slice enhancement is crucial for improving diagnostic accuracy and
enabling robust 3D reconstruction. This section details enhancement
methodologies using EHO, PSO, ACO, and GA, each tailored to address
noise suppression, contrast optimization, and structural preservation.
8.2.1 Algorithmic Approach for MRI Enhancement Using EHO
EHO optimized MRI enhancement by modeling pixel intensity refinement
as an optimization problem. Each elephant represented an enhancement
configuration balancing contrast and noise suppression. The initialization
phase generated intensity matrices within the 0–255 grayscale range for di-
verse exploration. Iterative updates followed:
Xnew = Xcurrent + r · (Xherd-best − Xglobal-best) (4)
where r regulates search intensity. Final images were normalized via
percentile-based rescaling, ensuring clinically relevant contrast. EHO’ s
adaptive strategy outperformed fixed-intensity methods, preserving fine
anatomical details while preventing excessive contrast distortions.
8.2.2 Adaptations for MRI Enhancement Using PSO
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) modeled pixel intensity adjustments as
particles refining enhancement solutions. The velocity update followed:
Vnew = ωVold + c1r1(Pbest − Xcurrent) + c2r2(Gbest − Xcurrent) (5)
where ω is inertia, and c1, c2 regulate cognitive and social influence. PSO’ s
reliance on global best solutions enabled fast convergence but struggled
with noise, leading to suboptimal enhancements in MRI slices with high
contrast variability.

8.2.4 Comparative Methodology for MRI Enhancement Using GA
Genetic Algorithm (GA) modeled MRI enhancement as an evolution-
ary process, encoding intensity refinements as chromosomes optimized
via selection, crossover, and mutation. Selective introduction of Gaus-
sian noise prevented premature convergence, while elitism preserved high-
performing solutions. A dynamic mutation rate balanced exploration and
exploitation. Though robust, GA converged significantly slower than EHO
and PSO, especially in noisy slices. Histogram-equalized initialization im-
proved performance, reducing noise susceptibility.

8.4.1 Methodology for Slice-by-Slice 3D Reconstruction
The methodology employs EHO, PSO, GA, and ACO, to individual MRI
slices which are enhanced, aligned and processed to generate accurate 3D
reconstructions, preserving critical anatomical features. The Marching

lineates structural boundaries which were visualised using the mayavi li-
brary, facilitating detailed rendering and interactive exploration. Ensur-
ing inter-slice continuity prevented artifacts, preserving the accurate spatial
structure of brain tissues.

8.5 Complete 3D Model Creation from All Slices
The full 3D brain reconstruction process aggregates all enhanced MRI slices
into a single volumetric model, providing a comprehensive view of anatom-
ical structures. Unlike the individual slice-based approach, this method
demands meticulous alignment of slices to preserve inter-slice continuity.
8.5.1 Framework for Full 3D Brain Reconstruction Using Enhanced
Slices
Enhanced slices processed through bio-inspired algorithms ensure that
structural integrity and contrast details are retained, making the volumetric
model more suitable for diagnostic and analytical applications.
3D reconstruction is TSP-reducible, where MRI slices act as nodes, and the
goal is to optimize traversal for anatomical continuity. The cost function,
based on inter-slice similarity metrics, ensures minimal distortion. Map-
ping this to TSP allows EHO to optimize slice ordering, reducing misalign-
ment and enhancing reconstruction fidelity.
The reconstruction pipeline employs sequential stacking of enhanced slices,
followed by Marching Cubes-based iso-surface extraction to generate a 3D
triangular mesh. Post-processing techniques, such as Gaussian smoothing,
are applied to refine surface details. The use of enhanced slices significantly
improves the quality of the reconstructed model, mitigating noise artifacts
and ensuring clearer differentiation of normal and pathological tissues.
8.5.2 Results and Observations: 3D Brain Reconstruction and Tumor
Detection
The final 3D models constructed from all slices revealed the superior per-
formance of EHO in producing anatomically accurate representations. The
method effectively maintained smooth surfaces and well-defined structural
boundaries while minimizing artifacts. PSO-based reconstructions, while
computationally efficient, introduced minor distortions due to premature
convergence. GA yielded high-contrast models but required longer pro-
cessing times, making it less viable for real-time applications.
T umor segmentation was successfully integrated into the reconstructed
models, enabling the identification of abnormal regions with high preci-
sion. The enhanced 3D visualization of tumors facilitates preoperative as-
sessment, allowing clinicians to analyze tumor size and spatial relationships
with surrounding structures. These findings highlight the potential of bio-
inspired enhancement techniques in advancing volumetric medical imag-
ing, offering a robust framework for both diagnostic and interventional ap-
