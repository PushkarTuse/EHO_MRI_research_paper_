The sociological intricacies of nature have inspired numerous optimisation algorithms, offering heuristic approaches to NP-hard and high-dimensional problems for which brute-force methods are impractical. Elephant Herding Optimisation (EHO), a bio-inspired metaheuristic, models hierarchical influences within elephant clans to balance exploration and exploitation through matriarch-guided updates and population diversification. This study proposes a modified EHO framework incorporating dimensionality-scaled position updates and adaptive control parameters to regulate search intensity across successive iterations. These modifications are designed to support convergence and maintain search diversity in complex optimisation spaces. A comparative analysis examines EHO alongside Genetic Algorithms (GA), Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO) and Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO) using the Travelling Salesman Problem, Vehicle Routing Problem, Rastrigin function and Ackley function, with attention to solution quality, convergence behaviour and execution time. The study extends this optimisation framework to computational medical diagnostics through MRI slice enhancement and three-dimensional reconstruction. MRI enhancement is formulated as an intensity-refinement problem that balances contrast adjustment, noise suppression and the preservation of anatomical structure. The imaging methodology combines slice processing and percentile-based intensity rescaling with volumetric assembly and Marching Cubes surface extraction to produce three-dimensional representations for visual analysis. Enhancement comparisons consider peak signal-to-noise ratio, structural similarity and processing time, placing image quality and computational requirements within a common evaluation framework. By connecting classical optimisation benchmarks with MRI processing, the study examines the practical role of hierarchical, population-based search in medical imaging and its potential to refine diagnostic workflows through enhanced slice visualisation and three-dimensional anatomical representation.
